THE JOB LAYER'S SHAPE, AND WHY
C's job layer is a table of asynchronous children whose pipes the loop's one poll(2) reads
beside the tty and the signal pipe, each job's consumer a callback (sink kinds BUFFER, CURSOR,
FILTER, CALLBACK with callback_ops for compgen/comphelp/compfish/shsession). wolf 0.2.33 waits
on ONE descriptor (os_signal_wait_fd), so no child pipe can be read while the loop runs (G2);
and no function value crosses a module boundary (G19/G20). The port therefore has:
- no job table at all — every asynchronous spawn refuses by name at its surface (above), so no
  child, pipe or zombie can exist outside a synchronous call;
- the callbacks as KINDS: each consumer is its own unit's refusal path (compgen's cg_spawn_for,
  comphelp_idle, compfish_idle, shsession_run_refusal, shell_*_refusal), and the arrival
  callback is cmdline_compgen_arrived called by name;
- one synchronous route, edit.job_run_sync: the standard environment (job_env; PAGER/GIT_PAGER
  kept for a terminal-owning child, 57.31 §3) and the workspace cwd set on the PROCESS around
  the spawn and restored after (G36: std.process has no set_cwd/set_env), SIGINT/SIGQUIT
  ignored in the editor while the child runs, the terminal handed over and back
  (tty_handover_begin/end, a resize after), stdin mapped, exec failure (ENOENT/EACCES) told
  from exit 127, a signal death reported as one.
- state as Ed fields (Ed.specs, Ed.gen, Ed.help, Ed.fish, Ed.comp.shell), C's file statics.
